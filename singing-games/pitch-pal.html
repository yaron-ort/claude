Good to know

  • Why not Spotify or YouTube directly? Those apps protect their audio, so no web page is allowed to listen to it. Use a song file you own (MP3, M4A, WAV), for example from a purchased download.
  • Best results: a song with a clear lead voice and light backing. An a cappella or "vocals only" version works best of all. Busy rock or heavy bass makes the singer's line patchy.
  • Wear headphones. Otherwise the mic hears the song too and gets confused. With Bluetooth headphones, sing into the phone's own mic: if the headset's mic is used, the phone switches to call mode and the music can stop. Pick the mic in the Microphone menu if needed. Wired headphones work best.
  • Any octave counts lets a low voice sing a high singer's song (or the other way round) one octave apart. Turn it off to practise the exact octave.
  • Start with the practice exercises: their notes are perfectly clean, so feedback is exact.
  • The singer's line ahead of the playhead shows what's coming, so you can prepare the next note.
